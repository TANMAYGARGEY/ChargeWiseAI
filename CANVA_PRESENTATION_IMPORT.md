### Slide 7: Mathematical Formulations (Vehicle Urgency Heuristic)
- **Category:** CORE ALGORITHM DETAIL
- **Title:** Mathematical Formulations: Vehicle Urgency Heuristic
- **Layout:** 2-column layout (Left: Mathematical breakdown, Right: Algorithmic justification).
- **Card 1 (Left) — The 3-Factor Urgency Score Formula:**
  - **Formula:**  
    $$U = 0.45 \times \text{LowBattery} + 0.30 \times \text{TightDeadline} + 0.25 \times \text{ChargePressure}$$
  - **1. Low Battery (45% weight):** $(100 - \text{Battery\%}) / 100$  
    *Purpose:* Heavily penalizes deep discharge; prevents vehicles from being stranded.
  - **2. Tight Deadline (30% weight):** $1.0 / \max(1, \text{Departure} - \text{Arrival})$  
    *Purpose:* Prioritizes vehicles departing soonest.
  - **3. Charge Pressure (25% weight):** $\text{SlotsNeeded} / \max(1, \text{Departure} - \text{Arrival})$  
    *Purpose:* Measures how tightly the charging duration packs into the available window.
- **Card 2 (Right) — Why This Heuristic Was Chosen:**
  - **Deterministic & Explainable:** Every score can be hand-verified and mathematically justified to fleet operators.
  - **Sub-Millisecond Computation:** Eliminates heavy training overhead and black-box neural network unpredictable behaviors.
  - **Graceful Degradation:** Handles edge cases (zero battery, same-slot departure) without divide-by-zero errors via $\max(1, \dots)$.
  - **Directly Drives Scheduling:** The Executor Agent uses this Urgency Score to order incoming vehicle allocations.
- **Teacher Counter-Question:**
  - *Q: "Why 45%, 30%, and 25%? How were these weights determined?"*
  - *A: "Derived from fleet operations priority: battery safety is non-negotiable (45%), followed by hard departure deadlines (30%), with session slack acting as the tie-breaker (25%)."*

### Slide 8: Candidate Slot Scoring & Decision Policies
- **Category:** OPTIMIZATION STRATEGY
- **Title:** Candidate Slot Scoring & Decision Policy Weights
- **Layout:** 2-column layout (Left: Candidate scoring formula, Right: 3 Policy modes).
- **Card 1 (Left) — Candidate Slot Scoring Mechanics:**
  - For each vehicle, the Executor Agent evaluates all feasible $(\text{charger}, \text{start\_slot})$ windows.
  - **Score Formula (Lower is Better):**  
    $$\text{Score} = \text{PriceComponent} + \text{WaitComponent} - \text{UrgencyBonus}$$
  - **Where:**
    - $\text{PriceComponent} = (\text{AvgPrice} / 12.0) \times W_{\text{price}}$
    - $\text{WaitComponent} = (\text{WaitSlots} / \text{AvailableWindow}) \times W_{\text{wait}}$
    - $\text{UrgencyBonus} = \text{Urgency} \times W_{\text{urgency}} \times \left(\frac{1}{1 + \text{DeadlineSlack}}\right)$
  - *Result:* The slot window minimizing the Score is allocated.
- **Card 2 (Right) — How the 3 Policies Shift Agent Priorities:**
  - **1. Balanced Policy (Standard Default):**  
    Weights: Price=1.0, Wait=0.55, Urgency=1.8. Balances off-peak savings with prompt vehicle turnaround.
  - **2. Cost Saver Policy (Economy Mode):**  
    Weights: Price=1.8, Wait=0.20, Urgency=0.45. Heavily penalizes peak slots (₹11.00); shifts sessions to off-peak (₹6.80).
  - **3. Urgent First Policy (Emergency / Rush Mode):**  
    Weights: Price=0.45, Wait=0.15, Urgency=3.2. Charges low SoC vehicles immediately, ignoring peak price surges.
- **Teacher Counter-Question:**
  - *Q: "Why use greedy heuristic allocation instead of a full MILP solver?"*
  - *A: "Real-world EV hubs need real-time, dynamic scheduling as vehicles arrive unexpectedly. Greedy priority allocation runs in O(N*C*S) time (~5ms), making it lightning fast and easily explainable without heavy proprietary solver licenses (e.g. Gurobi/CPLEX)."*

### Slide 9: The Independent Critic Agent & 6 Hard Constraints
- **Category:** SAFETY & VERIFICATION
- **Title:** The Critic Agent: Zero-Trust Constraint Validation
- **Layout:** 2-column layout (Left: 6 Constraints, Right: Decoupling Rationale).
- **Card 1 (Left) — The 6 Hard Physical Constraints Checked:**
  1. **Charger Exclusivity:** Max 1 EV per charger in any 30-min slot (no physical overlap).
  2. **Vehicle Exclusivity:** No vehicle can charge at two bays simultaneously.
  3. **Arrival Bound:** No charging slot is assigned prior to vehicle arrival.
  4. **Departure Deadline:** No charging slot is assigned at or after departure.
  5. **Power Intake Rating:** Assigned power (kW) $\le$ vehicle's configured hardware limit.
  6. **Planning Horizon:** All slots strictly within station horizon (0 to $N-1$).
- **Card 2 (Right) — Why Decouple the Critic from the Planner?:**
  - **Zero-Trust Safety:** The Planner uses heuristics that could theoretically have bugs or edge-case oversights. The Critic provides a hard mathematical barrier.
  - **Detailed Diagnostics:** The Critic outputs exact error strings (*'Charger conflict at Slot 2'*) so the Coordinator knows precisely why a plan failed.
  - **Regulatory Compliance:** In real energy grids, physical safety rules are non-negotiable. An agent must never be permitted to approve its own homework.
  - **Capacity Advisories:** If an EV cannot fit its full requested charge, Critic emits an informative warning while preserving schedule validity.
- **Teacher Counter-Question:**
  - *Q: "What is the difference between an Error and a Warning in the Critic?"*
  - *A: "An Error violates physical safety (e.g., two cars on one plug) and triggers a REJECT + Replan. A Warning indicates capacity bottleneck (e.g. EV received 80% instead of 100% due to short stay) but maintains schedule integrity."*
